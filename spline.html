What to notice

Degree in 1D

First degree joins points with straight segments. Higher-degree B-splines allow smoother joins and can bend differently between the same observations.

Order on the surface

The spatial choices are linear radial, thin-plate, and cubic radial kernels. Their order labels describe the kernel, not the transect’s polynomial degree.

Compare with IDW

IDW weights observations by distance. A radial spline solves for a surface that balances fit and smoothness; different kernels give different shapes.

Group discussion questions

Change one factor at a time. Compare predictions at the same location and record your group’s explanation in the shared Google Doc.

  1. 1D spline degreeTurn on degrees 1 through 5 together. Where do their curves differ most between observations? Does a higher degree always improve the fit?
  2. Smoothness and outliersWhich degree responds most strongly to the high outlier? What changes when you allow an approximate fit?
  3. Spatial spline orderCompare the 1st-, 2nd-, and 3rd-order surface kernels. Where do the maps and prediction values differ most?
  4. Global vs localHow does selecting only nearby observations change the experiment surface? Do local neighborhood boundaries leave seams?
  5. IDW vs splineWith the same observations, where do IDW and the spline differ most? How does their treatment of smoothness differ?
