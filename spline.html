What to notice

From curve to surface

The transect shows a one-dimensional cubic spline. The maps use a related, but different, two-dimensional thin-plate spline.

Exact or approximate

At λ = 0, spline estimates pass through observations. Smoothing trades exact fit for a gentler surface.

Compare with IDW

Both methods are deterministic by default. IDW weights observations by distance; the spline solves for a surface that balances fit and smoothness.

Group discussion questions

Change one factor at a time. Compare the transect and maps, then record your group's explanation in the shared Google Doc.

  1. Global vs localWhere does using only the nearest k samples change the surface most? Do local neighborhood boundaries leave visible seams?
  2. Exact vs approximateWhich observations does a smoothed spline miss? When might accepting a small miss be useful?
  3. Gradual vs abruptHow does a smooth spline behave across a sharp transition? Where would you trust the prediction least?
  4. Deterministic vs stochasticWhich map repeats exactly with the same settings? What does one simulated realization tell you, and what can it not tell you?
  5. IDW vs splineWith the same observations, where do IDW and the spline differ most? How does their treatment of smoothness differ?

The transect is a one-dimensional cubic spline; the spatial maps use a two-dimensional thin-plate spline. The stochastic view is an illustrative simulation, not kriging or a probability estimate. Values are fictional.
